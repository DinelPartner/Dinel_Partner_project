Choosing an EV charger is about more than picking the sleekest-looking box in the shop. What matters most is that the charger is correctly sized for your car's needs and your property's electrical capacity. An electrician who properly assesses your main fuse and electrical panel before installation saves you both money and future headaches.

Always choose a charger with a type B residual-current device (RCD) or equivalent protection, since EV charging has different earth-fault protection requirements than ordinary power outlets. Also consider where the unit is placed — close to the car's charging port, sheltered from the weather, with enough cable length, but still a safe distance from anything flammable.

Last but not least: installation should always be carried out by a licensed electrician and reported to your grid operator. That ensures your warranty is valid, your insurance covers the installation, and that you actually get the power output you need — without risking an overload in your property's electrical system.
